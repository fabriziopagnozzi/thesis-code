## Open Questions (self)

- **Task justification:** For which task is this dataset the right fit? We need a clear explanation of why the MIMIC benchmark is built and what purpose the generated queries serve.
- **Circular reasoning risk:** If we generate queries designed to favor coverage, then evaluate coverage, the result is tautological. Counterargument needed — perhaps: the queries reflect a realistic clinical information need (multi-aspect comorbidity questions), and coverage is just one of several retrieval strategies tested on them. The queries are not *designed for* coverage; they are *designed to be realistic*, and coverage happens to handle them better.
- **Multi-aspect query combinatorics:** Since the final QA answer is not our primary evaluation target (we focus on retrieval quality), we could generate multi-aspect queries covering up to 4 aspects and enumerate combinations. This produces a large comorbidity space — too large for humans to reason about, but tractable computationally. An LLM, given all relevant chunks via coverage-aware retrieval, can cross-check co-occurrences and produce good responses. Key: reason carefully about query design.
- **Benchmark design conventions:** How does the literature approach query generation for these tasks on clinical data? Many benchmarks evaluate document ranking (nDCG@k) rather than answer generation. Our case is different: we want to assess not just document relevance but the quality of aspect coverage for multi-aspect queries, so nDCG@k alone is insufficient.
